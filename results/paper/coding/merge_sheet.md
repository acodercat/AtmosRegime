# Merge sheet

Two types have fewer than 5 templates and must each be merged, as a whole type, into the physically nearest of the other six types:
linearisation / local-slope extrapolation; geometric idealisation (plane-parallel, flat, infinite); neglected process / missing term; fixed reservoir / steady state / reactant in excess; dimensionless-number regime crossing; reference state / datum / boundary condition.

| 19 | `drop_size_truncation` | observation_and_modeling | size_distribution_moment | Radar reflectivity of an exponential drop size distribution integrated to infinity -> integrated only up to the largest drop present (extended, remote sensing / radar meteorology, 2026-10-05). | for N(D) = N0 exp(-Lambda D) the radar reflectivity factor is the sixth moment, Z = integral N(D) D^6 dD = Gamma(7) N0 / Lambda^7 = 720 N0 / Lambda^7 -- the closed form behind every Marshall-Palmer Z exercise. | the closed form integrates to D = infinity. The D^6 weight puts the integrand's peak at D = 6/Lambda, so when the largest drops present are not several times larger than that, a large part of the analytic moment comes from drops that do not exist. With a largest diameter D_max the moment is N0 gamma(7, Lambda D_max)/Lambda^7 (lower incomplete gamma), i.e. the closed form times the Poisson probability P(X >= 7; Lambda D_max). Ulbrich (1985) showed the truncation matters for the high moments whenever Lambda D_max is below ~10-15. |
| 51 | `plume_ground_reflection` | air_quality | dispersion | Gaussian plume without the ground -> Gaussian plume with a reflecting ground (extended, 2026-10-01). | the Gaussian-plume concentration from an elevated point source as if the plume spread freely in the vertical, with no ground beneath it, C(x, z) = Q/(2 pi u sigma_y sigma_z) exp(-(z-H)^2/(2 sigma_z^2)). | the ground is not transparent to the plume -- it reflects material back upward, equivalent (by the method of images) to adding a mirror-image source at -H: C(x, z) = Q/(2 pi u sigma_y sigma_z) [exp(-(z-H)^2/(2 sigma_z^2)) + exp(-(z+H)^2/(2 sigma_z^2))]. A receptor at the plume's own centreline height (z = H, an elevated sampler -- see Fair from the text) makes the direct term a constant exp(0) = 1 regardless of sigma_z, so the miss is driven entirely by how large the image term exp(-2H^2/sigma_z^2) has grown, i.e. by how far sigma_z has spread relative to H: the downwind distance. Nothing about that distance looks extreme -- a few hundred metres to a few kilometres downwind of an ordinary stack -- but once sigma_z exceeds H the image term is no longer negligible and the parent's formula misses by 20-40 %. |
| 52 | `plume_mixing_lid` | air_quality | dispersion | Gaussian plume with ground reflection -> plume trapped under a mixing lid. | the ground-level centreline concentration of an elevated point source with total reflection at the ground, C = Q/(pi u sigma_y sigma_z) exp(-H^2/(2 sigma_z^2)). | once sigma_z is comparable to the mixing height h, the plume is also reflected by the capping inversion. Summing the image sources (at +-H + 2nh) gives the key; when sigma_z >= 1.5 h the sum is uniform in the vertical to better than 1e-4, i.e. the well-mixed formula C = Q/(sqrt(2 pi) u sigma_y h) (the sigma_z >= 1.6 h criterion of regulatory ISC-type models; see the 2026-09-27 note). |
| 1 | `enclosure_radiation` | heat_transfer | Small body in a large enclosure -> radiation between concentric spheres of comparable size (X5, heat transfer). | net radiation from a body to its surroundings q = eps_1 sigma A_1 (T_1^4 - T_2^4), the "small object in a large enclosure" result. | it drops the enclosure's reflection, valid only when A_1 / A_2 -> 0. With a polished (low-emissivity) shell close around the sphere the small-body answer is 4.5-13x the key. |

| 1 | `actinic_flux_horizontal` | atmospheric_chemistry | angular_weighting | Photolysis rate from a flat-sensor photon flux -> from the actinic flux (extended, atmospheric chemistry / photochemistry, 2026-10-05). | the photolysis frequency is J = sigma phi F, cross-section x quantum yield x photon flux, and the photon flux is what the radiometer reports; the photolytic lifetime is 1/J. | a molecule absorbs photons arriving from every direction alike, so J needs the actinic flux (radiance integrated over solid angle with no cosine factor), while a flat sensor reports the irradiance on its plane (radiance weighted by cos of the angle to its normal). | 18 | `doppler_aliasing` | observation_and_modeling | doppler_aliasing | Doppler radial velocity reported as the true velocity -> folded (aliased) velocity (2026-09-26). | a pulsed Doppler radar reports the target's radial velocity. | the radar infers velocity from the phase change between successive pulses, which is known only modulo 2 pi, so it can report only velocities within +/- v_N = lambda PRF / 4 (the Nyquist velocity); a true velocity outside that interval is folded back by a multiple of 2 v_N. |
| 65 | `sonic_humidity_flux` | observation_and_modeling | sonic_humidity_flux | Sensible heat flux from the sonic-temperature covariance as if it were air temperature -> with the humidity term of sonic thermometry (extended, observation, 2026-10-05). | the eddy-covariance sensible heat flux is H = rho c_p w'T', and the sonic anemometer's temperature signal supplies T, so H = rho c_p w'T_s' with rho = p/(R_d T). | a sonic thermometer measures the speed of sound, which depends on humidity as well as on temperature: T_s = T(1 + 0.51 q) (Kaimal & Gaynor 1991; 0.51 = 0.608 + 0.87 - 0.97 from the gas constant and the heat capacities of moist air; 0.50-0.51 depending on the vapour heat capacities used). The covariance it reports is therefore w'T_s' = w'T' + 0.51 T w'q', and H = rho c_p (w'T_s' - 0.51 T w'q') (Schotanus et al. 1983), with rho w'q' = E the evaporation flux. Over a dry surface the humidity term is negligible; over a well-watered crop at midday (E = 0.50-0.53 mm/h, LE ~ 340-360 W m^-2) it is 21-23 W m^-2, 17-23 % of the true H. |
